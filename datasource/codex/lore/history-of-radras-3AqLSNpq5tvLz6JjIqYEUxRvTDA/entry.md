Radras is a maritime monarchy on the warm western coast of the central continent. Where Althania’s strength is its rebuilt institutions and disciplined land armies, Radras survives through ships, ports, contracts, and the ability to keep trade—and therefore food and steel—moving even when the continent burns. Its friendship with Althania is not sentimental; it is strategic, long-practiced, and mutually necessary.

1) THE RED COAST AND THE FIRST CROWN

Before it was a kingdom, Radras was a chain of rival coastal city-states wedged between iron-red cliffs and wide river deltas. Harbor lords feuded over docking rights, salt flats, and inland timber routes. Unification came under Rhadros the Oathbound, a warlord who conquered the coast and then bound it together through marriages and a permanent council that recorded obligations like law. That tradition became the Crown Chart: Radras’ habit of treating politics as written contract, not personal favor. The monarchy endures because it never forgets that ports only stay rich when peace is maintained.

2) RADRAS IN THE GREAT HUMAN–DEMON WAR

When the human–demon war intensified, Radras learned that demons didn’t need to conquer farmland to cripple a realm—they only had to choke routes. Inland roads became unsafe as monsters spread, and coastal raids turned frequent. Radras responded with sea-born doctrine: beacon-forts along cliff lines, the Waveguard navy to fight pirates and monsters, and mage-service embedded into sailing (ward-scribes, stormbreakers, signal-lantern mages).

At the same time, the wider demon race had already suffered a crucial blow: the Demon Lords had been slain by the ancient Heroes, leaving demons powerful but less unified. Demon strategy turned brutal and uneven—mass invasions and terror campaigns rather than clean, centralized conquest. Radras exploited that weakness by refusing “glory battles.” Instead, it protected arteries: it kept ports open, escorted grain ships, and ensured the continent still had routes that worked.

3) THE CROWNLESS YEARS AND THE QUIET ALLIANCE WITH ALTHANIA

When Althania’s old royal line was slaughtered and its capital burned, refugees poured out—commoners, minor nobles, and displaced soldiers. Many landed in Radras’ harbors. Radras did not openly seize Althania’s ruined lands; occupying shattered inland territory would be costly, and Radras’ court feared something worse than expansion: a fractured human south that would invite another demon surge.

So Radras helped without making itself the story.

- Merchant houses quietly funneled grain, iron, and timber into frontier markets that still resisted.

- Diplomats hosted “trade arbitrations” that were really negotiations between displaced Althanian nobles and surviving commanders.

- The Western Church received safe coastal passage, helping holy knights move where they were needed.

By the time Arbalest, Roosevelt, and Alfrost began rallying the remnants of Althania, Radras was already treating their coalition like a future state—supplying it, legitimizing it, and warning other human powers not to prey on the ruins.

4) AFTER THE DEMON MASSACRE: WHY RADRAS CLUNG TO ALTHANIA

When the counteroffensive finally drove demon forces north and the wasteland slaughter ended the campaign, the demons adapted in a way that alarmed Radras’ strategists: instead of one Demon Lord, they created multiple Demon Lords, each ruling a separate region with its own army and capital. That system made the north harder to predict—several threats could rise at once, each using different tactics: raids, corruption, sabotage, or “peace” offers meant to divide human courts.

Radras concluded that survival required alliances that outlast individual rulers. This is when the modern Radras–Althania relationship hardened into a formal framework often called the Harbor-Crown Accord:

A) Mutual Defense Understanding

Radras provides ships, coastal mobility, and supplies when demon pressure rises; Althania provides disciplined land forces and command structure.

B) Trade as Infrastructure

Radras becomes Althania’s door to the sea. Althania becomes Radras’ safest inland corridor. Both invest in keeping roads, escort routes, and border garrisons functional.

C) Permanent Diplomacy

Radras establishes a long-term embassy in Arbalest (nicknamed the Red Quarters for its dyes and banners), staffed not only by nobles but by contract-scribes trained to lock promises into airtight language. Althania returns the gesture with a resident envoy in Radras’ cliff-palace capital, Veylharbor, where shipyards are carved directly into stone.

Over generations, marriage ties form—not always direct heirs, but enough that both courts treat the alliance as “family-adjacent.” Even when other kingdoms try to pull Radras away, Radras answers with the same logic: Althania is the shield that keeps the south from becoming a battlefield again.

5) RADRAS TODAY

Radras is polite, cultured, and endlessly negotiable—until the ink dries. Its diplomacy favors written commitments and carefully framed concessions. A Radras envoy will smile while trapping rivals inside their own wording, and Radras guilds can move supplies faster than some kingdoms can raise levies.

Radras’ strengths:

- Navy-first power that can reposition troops and cargo quickly.

- Merchant guild influence integrated into governance.

- Information networks that hear rumors before palaces do.

Radras stays close to Althania because the age of one Demon Lord is gone; the age of many is harder to contain. If Althania fractures under internal succession strain, demons may not need a direct invasion—agents, timing, and divided humans could do the work.

If Althania is the rebuilt crown of the south, Radras is the anchor beside it—steadying the region so the next storm doesn’t take them both.

- House Reynhardt (minor noble line tied to House Alfrost)

- House Background:

House Reynhardt is the ruling noble-mayor family of Calderidge, an Althanian agricultural hub town that many travelers pass through.

Selene was born into House Reynhardt and lived as a minor noble of Calderidge before leaving the capital-side noble sphere and marrying into the Astrea household.

Their authority is local governance rather than high court power, so they manage town administration, order, and public duties on behalf of the crown’s regional structure.

Their tie to House Alfrost is political and administrative, placing Reynhardt under Alfrost’s influence and protection as part of the southern region’s noble network.
