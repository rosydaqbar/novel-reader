### Divine Beings

**Type:** character
**Source:** `characters/divine-beings-3ApoTG9qpzfyeQqDxAfrYFZzYAu/entry.md`
**Aliases:** Divine, Beings
**Tags:** None
**Context:** alwaysIncludeInContext=true, doNotTrack=false, noAutoInclude=false

Higher-dimensional entities who oversee reincarnation and the balance between worlds.

- Purpose: To guide souls into new lives when their previous ones have reached an endpoint of destiny or consequence.

- Reincarnation Process:

When a person is resurrected by the Divine Beings, their memories of their previous life are completely erased. However, they receive something known as a Divine Blessing — a gift reflecting the essence of their past life's talent or virtue.

These Blessings often manifest as innate abilities, instincts, or affinities, though the recipients rarely understand their origin. They only retain a vague “feeling” of familiarity toward their skill or strength.

- Elrond/Saitou’s Exception:

Among the countless reincarnated souls, Elrond von Astrea is a unique anomaly. Due to his photographic memory, his mind resisted the erasure process, allowing him to retain every memory from his past life as Saitou.

This unprecedented event deeply unsettled many Divine Beings, as such memory retention was believed impossible. Most viewed it as a breach of natural law—an imbalance to the reincarnation cycle.

However, the Divine Being who personally resurrected Saitou opposed their judgment, declaring:

“You are the only hope.”

The meaning of “hope” remains a mystery, known only to that Divine Being. Whether it refers to Elrond’s role in the new world or a greater purpose tied to both realms is unknown.

### History of Althania

**Type:** lore
**Source:** `lore/history-of-althania-3AqLOm690CvD4Pt210u9ndupwjk/entry.md`
**Aliases:** None
**Tags:** Althania
**Context:** alwaysIncludeInContext=false, doNotTrack=false, noAutoInclude=false

Althania’s modern history begins with collapse. The **old Althania royal bloodline** ruled from a different capital until the great human–demon war reached its worst point. By then, the demons had already **lost their single unifying leadership—the Demon Lords—killed by the Heroes of the past**. Even without Demon Lords, the remaining demon armies were still strong enough to invade south, and in that invasion the **Althania royal family was slaughtered**, the capital burned, and the crown vanished. What followed became known as the **Crownless Years**: Althania had **no king**, and the demon race also had **no Demon Lord**, leaving both sides unstable—roads grew unsafe, monsters spread, and borderlands were left to survive on their own.

Out of that disorder, three noble houses moved first—**Arbalest, Roosevelt, and Alfrost**—later remembered as the **Founding Families**. They rallied scattered nobles and frontier garrisons, rebuilding an army from whatever the kingdom could still raise. The war only truly turned when two decisive forces joined them: the **last descendants of the Heroes’ party** (still carrying blessed blood) and the **holy knights of the Western Church**. Under one banner, humans pushed north, reclaiming towns and fortresses and driving the demon host back toward the wasteland border.

The end of the campaign became infamous as the **Demon Massacre**, when retreating demons were caught and crushed—especially once **elven forces** struck from other directions with superior magic and archery. The demons survived, but the loss was so severe—coming after the earlier fall of the Demon Lords—that they reshaped their rule: instead of risking everything on **one Demon Lord**, they created **multiple Demon Lords**, each controlling a region, army, and capital, making them harder to eliminate in one decisive heroic strike.

With the demons forced into the far north beyond a harsh wasteland/desert barrier, humans and elves chose diplomacy and alliance against the demon race. In the south, Althania rebuilt: **House Arbalest took the throne**, renamed the capital **Arbalest**, but kept the kingdom’s name **Althania** to honor the slain royal line. That peace endured for centuries—until the present era, where **King Aldren von Arbalest II’s** fatal curse-illness makes succession urgent, splitting the court into rival factions around **Lucien** and **Darius**, with **Claire’s** alignment capable of tipping the great houses and breaking the balance.

### History of Radras

**Type:** lore
**Source:** `lore/history-of-radras-3AqLSNpq5tvLz6JjIqYEUxRvTDA/entry.md`
**Aliases:** None
**Tags:** Radras
**Context:** alwaysIncludeInContext=false, doNotTrack=false, noAutoInclude=false

Radras is a maritime monarchy on the warm western coast of the central continent. Where Althania’s strength is its rebuilt institutions and disciplined land armies, Radras survives through ships, ports, contracts, and the ability to keep trade—and therefore food and steel—moving even when the continent burns. Its friendship with Althania is not sentimental; it is strategic, long-practiced, and mutually necessary.

1) THE RED COAST AND THE FIRST CROWN

Before it was a kingdom, Radras was a chain of rival coastal city-states wedged between iron-red cliffs and wide river deltas. Harbor lords feuded over docking rights, salt flats, and inland timber routes. Unification came under Rhadros the Oathbound, a warlord who conquered the coast and then bound it together through marriages and a permanent council that recorded obligations like law. That tradition became the Crown Chart: Radras’ habit of treating politics as written contract, not personal favor. The monarchy endures because it never forgets that ports only stay rich when peace is maintained.

2) RADRAS IN THE GREAT HUMAN–DEMON WAR

When the human–demon war intensified, Radras learned that demons didn’t need to conquer farmland to cripple a realm—they only had to choke routes. Inland roads became unsafe as monsters spread, and coastal raids turned frequent. Radras responded with sea-born doctrine: beacon-forts along cliff lines, the Waveguard navy to fight pirates and monsters, and mage-service embedded into sailing (ward-scribes, stormbreakers, signal-lantern mages).

At the same time, the wider demon race had already suffered a crucial blow: the Demon Lords had been slain by the ancient Heroes, leaving demons powerful but less unified. Demon strategy turned brutal and uneven—mass invasions and terror campaigns rather than clean, centralized conquest. Radras exploited that weakness by refusing “glory battles.” Instead, it protected arteries: it kept ports open, escorted grain ships, and ensured the continent still had routes that worked.

3) THE CROWNLESS YEARS AND THE QUIET ALLIANCE WITH ALTHANIA

When Althania’s old royal line was slaughtered and its capital burned, refugees poured out—commoners, minor nobles, and displaced soldiers. Many landed in Radras’ harbors. Radras did not openly seize Althania’s ruined lands; occupying shattered inland territory would be costly, and Radras’ court feared something worse than expansion: a fractured human south that would invite another demon surge.

So Radras helped without making itself the story.

- Merchant houses quietly funneled grain, iron, and timber into frontier markets that still resisted.

- Diplomats hosted “trade arbitrations” that were really negotiations between displaced Althanian nobles and surviving commanders.

- The Western Church received safe coastal passage, helping holy knights move where they were needed.

By the time Arbalest, Roosevelt, and Alfrost began rallying the remnants of Althania, Radras was already treating their coalition like a future state—supplying it, legitimizing it, and warning other human powers not to prey on the ruins.

4) AFTER THE DEMON MASSACRE: WHY RADRAS CLUNG TO ALTHANIA

When the counteroffensive finally drove demon forces north and the wasteland slaughter ended the campaign, the demons adapted in a way that alarmed Radras’ strategists: instead of one Demon Lord, they created multiple Demon Lords, each ruling a separate region with its own army and capital. That system made the north harder to predict—several threats could rise at once, each using different tactics: raids, corruption, sabotage, or “peace” offers meant to divide human courts.

Radras concluded that survival required alliances that outlast individual rulers. This is when the modern Radras–Althania relationship hardened into a formal framework often called the Harbor-Crown Accord:

A) Mutual Defense Understanding

Radras provides ships, coastal mobility, and supplies when demon pressure rises; Althania provides disciplined land forces and command structure.

B) Trade as Infrastructure

Radras becomes Althania’s door to the sea. Althania becomes Radras’ safest inland corridor. Both invest in keeping roads, escort routes, and border garrisons functional.

C) Permanent Diplomacy

Radras establishes a long-term embassy in Arbalest (nicknamed the Red Quarters for its dyes and banners), staffed not only by nobles but by contract-scribes trained to lock promises into airtight language. Althania returns the gesture with a resident envoy in Radras’ cliff-palace capital, Veylharbor, where shipyards are carved directly into stone.

Over generations, marriage ties form—not always direct heirs, but enough that both courts treat the alliance as “family-adjacent.” Even when other kingdoms try to pull Radras away, Radras answers with the same logic: Althania is the shield that keeps the south from becoming a battlefield again.

5) RADRAS TODAY

Radras is polite, cultured, and endlessly negotiable—until the ink dries. Its diplomacy favors written commitments and carefully framed concessions. A Radras envoy will smile while trapping rivals inside their own wording, and Radras guilds can move supplies faster than some kingdoms can raise levies.

Radras’ strengths:

- Navy-first power that can reposition troops and cargo quickly.

- Merchant guild influence integrated into governance.

- Information networks that hear rumors before palaces do.

Radras stays close to Althania because the age of one Demon Lord is gone; the age of many is harder to contain. If Althania fractures under internal succession strain, demons may not need a direct invasion—agents, timing, and divided humans could do the work.

If Althania is the rebuilt crown of the south, Radras is the anchor beside it—steadying the region so the next storm doesn’t take them both.

### Roxarea

**Type:** lore
**Source:** `lore/roxarea-3AqLWRWF20aavaS8MBJjOFeHdhf/entry.md`
**Aliases:** None
**Tags:** Roxarea
**Context:** alwaysIncludeInContext=false, doNotTrack=false, noAutoInclude=false

Overview:

Roxarea is a small border nation positioned at the northern buffer zone near the great northern desert, where human, elf, demon, and beastkin influences press against one another.

It is widely known as a “crossroads country”: a trade hub, a staging ground for adventurers, and a diplomatic pressure valve between rival regions.

Borders / Geography:

- North: the Northern Buffer Zone leading toward Demon Land (desert-edge, unstable frontier)

- Southwest: mountain ranges that mark the edge of Elven territory

- South: direct access routes to Althania and Radras

- Southeast: Beastkin lands

Roxarea’s terrain is mixed—fertile valleys and irrigated farmland clustered around trade roads, with harsh desert winds and monster zones pushing in from the north.

Origin (Before Serelis):

Before Serelis ruled, Roxarea was effectively lawless: a contested buffer strip where smugglers, warbands, mercenaries, and displaced clans fought over routes and water.

No lasting crown held it, because any local ruler was crushed by neighboring powers or consumed by internal violence.

Transformation (Rule by Force):

Thousands of years ago, Serelis Thalanor seized Roxarea by overwhelming force and imposed a single, absolute order at the “tip” of the country’s power structure.

Under her rule, Roxarea shifted from a chaotic buffer into a controlled neutral frontier state—feared enough to be respected, stable enough to be profitable.

Government:

Roxarea is a monarchy centered on a single immortal guardian sovereign:

- Serelis Thalanor (Elder Elf) — monarch and living deterrent

Beneath the throne, administration is practical and trade-focused: road security, customs control, border permits, and strict crime enforcement to keep the crossroads functioning.

Capital / Center:

The central city (often simply called “Roxarea City”) is built around the monarch’s palace complex, which sits at the nation’s midpoint like an anchor.

The palace is both seat of rule and symbol: a reminder that the country’s stability is not negotiated—it is enforced.

Economy:

Roxarea thrives despite its small size by leveraging what larger nations avoid:

- Trade nexus: merchants move goods between human lands, beastkin territories, and elf-adjacent routes

- Adventurer staging: a common launch point for expeditions toward rare material zones and border ruins

- Agriculture: an “agricultural empire in miniature,” using fertile pockets and controlled irrigation to export high-value crops

Signature product:

- A pomegranate-like fruit used to produce the region’s most prized liquor, exported across nearby nations as a luxury good

Monsters / Adventuring Value:

The northern influence and mixed terrain create unusual monster diversity, including rare species that drop high-grade materials.

This fuels a cycle:

- adventurers hunt → materials boost local crafts/trade → the economy grows → more adventurers arrive

Roxarea is considered one of the best places to obtain rare components without committing to full wasteland entry.

Reputation:

To outsiders, Roxarea is paradoxical:

- A border state that should be in constant collapse, yet remains stable for centuries

- A nation where many cultures mix freely, yet crime is punished swiftly

- A place where travelers feel safe… until they remember who is watching over it

Core Truth:

Roxarea’s stability is not the result of diplomacy alone.

It is the product of a single ruler whose existence makes invasion, sabotage, and prolonged disorder too costly to attempt.
