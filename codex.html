Saitou

- Age: 20

- Origin World: Earth

- Occupation (before death): Mathematician

- Traits: Photographic memory, analytical, perfectionist

- Cause of Death: Overwork at his desk after realizing his research led to humanity’s downfall

- Background:

Saitou was a young mathematical genius devoted to solving the most complex equations of human advancement. His groundbreaking work in theoretical mathematics became the foundation for a revolutionary new energy system—one later twisted into a devastating weapon of mass destruction.

The same discovery meant to guide humanity into prosperity instead annihilated much of his world. Overwhelmed by guilt, Saitou continued his research endlessly, desperate to find a way to reverse the damage. Exhausted and broken, he eventually collapsed at his desk.

Out of pity and respect, the Divine Beings offered him a second chance—to live again in another world, not as a punishment, but as an opportunity to rebuild his life free from the burden of his past mistakes.

Divine Beings

Higher-dimensional entities who oversee reincarnation and the balance between worlds.

- Purpose: To guide souls into new lives when their previous ones have reached an endpoint of destiny or consequence.

- Reincarnation Process:

When a person is resurrected by the Divine Beings, their memories of their previous life are completely erased. However, they receive something known as a Divine Blessing — a gift reflecting the essence of their past life's talent or virtue.

These Blessings often manifest as innate abilities, instincts, or affinities, though the recipients rarely understand their origin. They only retain a vague “feeling” of familiarity toward their skill or strength.

- Elrond/Saitou’s Exception:

Among the countless reincarnated souls, Elrond von Astrea is a unique anomaly. Due to his photographic memory, his mind resisted the erasure process, allowing him to retain every memory from his past life as Saitou.

This unprecedented event deeply unsettled many Divine Beings, as such memory retention was believed impossible. Most viewed it as a breach of natural law—an imbalance to the reincarnation cycle.

However, the Divine Being who personally resurrected Saitou opposed their judgment, declaring:

“You are the only hope.”

The meaning of “hope” remains a mystery, known only to that Divine Being. Whether it refers to Elrond’s role in the new world or a greater purpose tied to both realms is unknown.

Elrond von Astrea

Real Identity: Reincarnation of Saitou; his past-life memories persist instead of fading, making his “reincarnation” closer to a full continuity of self than a fresh start.

- Appearance: Black hair, fair skin, lean build that reads more “trained” than “muscular.” Usually seen in practical frontier clothing.

- Personality: Calm, logical, and careful about timing—he acts only after he has a working model of what he’s dealing with. In public, he can deliberately present himself as a normal, polite child even when his inner thoughts are far more adult.

- Unique Trait: Photographic memory carried over into his new life, letting him retain and organize past-life knowledge and new-world observations with unusual precision.

- Background:

Born and raised in the southern frontier outskirts of Althania as the son of Aldric and Selene.

His home life is stable by “capital standards,” but it is not truly distant from danger: monster activity and defense infrastructure problems are part of the region’s reality.

He grows up watching how his parents’ work holds the town together, and he starts training early with the goal of being strong enough to survive what the frontier (and later the capital) will demand.

His Divine Blessing is an anomaly: it remains unknown and unclear even from the Divine Beings’ side.

- Core Skills / Combat Direction:

Learns structured magic through principles first, then expands into enhancement by treating his own body like measurable data.

Trains toward a spellsword foundation (magic control + blade work), shaped by Aldric’s combat realism and Selene’s insistence on control before power.

- Current Trajectory / Goal:

After repeated exposure to real threats, he commits to entering the Royal Academy as the next necessary step rather than staying in the frontier.

- Key Relationships (as shown on-page):

Aldric von Astrea (father), Selene von Astrea (mother), Mira, Celica, and later Wilhelm Alfrost.

During travel, he forms early rapport with Lysette Farrow and Rina Calder.

Prince Lucien von Arbalest III

- Title: First Prince of the Althania Kingdom

- Age: Early 20s

- Appearance: Short black hair similar to Emilia’s, fair skin, composed expression

- Personality: Calm, thoughtful, and wise; embodies the dignity expected of the next king

- Background:

Lucien is the first prince and the official heir apparent to the throne of Althania. Personally educated by the King and senior royal advisors, he developed a deep understanding of governance, diplomacy, and reform.

Skilled in both swordsmanship and high-level magic, Lucien exemplifies the ideal royal heir—disciplined, balanced, and strategic.

Known for his sense of duty and rational leadership, Lucien was originally uninterested in forming political factions within the court. However, the rise of the Second Prince Faction led by his younger brother Darius forced him to take action.

Under pressure from nobles and advisors, Lucien established the First Prince Faction—not out of ambition, but as a necessary counterbalance to maintain political stability and protect the legitimacy of royal succession.

Despite his calm nature, Lucien bears the burden of constant political tension. He strives to preserve unity within the royal family while maintaining order among nobles who see his position as a source of influence.

His approach to conflict is measured and diplomatic, preferring reform and discussion over direct confrontation, yet he possesses the strategic insight to act decisively when the stability of the kingdom is threatened.

Prince Darius von Arbalest III

- Title: Second Prince of the Althania Kingdom

- Age: Early 17s

- Appearance: Bright yellow hair and blue eyes, resembling the King more than his siblings

- Personality: Intelligent, ambitious, and proud; often impulsive and driven by emotion

- Background:

Darius is the second prince, talented in both sword and magic but overshadowed by his elder brother Lucien. Though not chosen as heir, he seeks recognition through his strength and influence.

Fueled by frustration and ambition, Darius formed the Second Prince Faction, attracting nobles who shared his vision of radical progress and personal gain. Unlike Lucien, who values diplomacy, Darius believes that power and control are the true path to leadership.

His fascination with power led him down a darker path—secretly studying forbidden magic and gathering individuals blessed with powerful Divine Blessings under his influence.

He views the Divine Blessing not as a divine gift, but as a tool—a means to rule and reshape the kingdom under his own image.

His growing network of Blessed individuals and forbidden scholars has become a source of unease within the royal court, with rumors that his faction’s power rivals even the royal knights.

Though misguided, Darius genuinely believes his actions are for the kingdom’s future, convinced that only through dominance can true order be achieved.

Cestina Ludwell

- Position: Emilia’s aide (but regarded as a friend)

- Appearance: Long silky blonde hair, purple eyes, graceful elegance

- Personality: Calm, reasonable, soft-spoken

- Background :

Cestina comes from House Ludwell—a displaced noble line that lost its footing in an overseas conflict, crossed into Althania, and was taken in as an Alfrost branch family under several terms of service.

She and Emilia became best friends since they were five, but Cestina’s upbringing carried a hidden purpose: her beauty and presence could mislead a crowd, and at formal events she was trained to mimic Emilia’s etiquette, handwriting, routines, and even speech cadence, so she could draw eyes away when danger moved.

Rumors in ballrooms sometimes mistook her as “the Alfrost daughter,” and the household quietly exploited that confusion—until Emilia discovered the truth at seven and refused it. She refused it for one simple reason: Cestina was never a tool to her—not a body double, not a guardian, not a shield—just her best friend, and Emilia would rather risk herself than let Cestina be used as a substitute.

Emilia then pushed herself into public appearances so substitution became impossible without scandal; after that, Cestina’s role was rewritten from “double” into “guardian,” keeping her close for protection without forcing her to be a sacrificial stand-in.

Later, her composure and quiet strength captured Gilbert’s heart, and she became his fiancée—not as a fragile noble beauty, but as someone who endured the stage meant to break her and still chose loyalty without bitterness.

Arbalest

The massive capital of the Althania Kingdom is surrounded by towering white walls and numerous watchtowers bristling with pennants. The city is organized into distinct wards, featuring pale stone roadways that are cleaner and smoother than those found on the frontier. The main entrance is the Arbalest Main Gate, where all travelers and carriages must pass over a carved magic detection circle that pulses with light to screen for dark magic, curses, or unknown energies

The Royal District

An elite sector of Arbalest characterized by wide streets, tall stone walls, and manicured parks with shrubs trimmed into orderly shapes. It is home to the grand estates of the Three Founding Families and is heavily patrolled by guards in polished armor

Alfrost Mansion

Located near the central roundabouts of the Royal District, this official noble residence features elegant white walls and a large entrance path wide enough for multiple carriages. The interior includes a wide lobby with polished granite floors and a grand stained-glass family crest. It is divided into several wings: the main family rooms, a guest and aide wing, and an office wing containing Gilbert’s dark-wooded command office. The grounds include an East Courtyard for sword practice and an Upper Practice Hall for magic training, characterized by frost-marked stones from previous lessons

The Industrial District

A bustling sector of the capital filled with foundries, warehouses, and tall chimneys that blur the light with smoke. The air in this district carries the distinct, biting smell of coal, hot metal, and ink

The Business District

A lively area packed with food stalls, street performers, and a busy nightlife. It features a central fountain square where several roads intersect, leading toward the library and the adventurer hub

Adventurer Bureau

A large building with an entirely open front wall that faces the street, allowing the sounds of leather, metal, and dust to spill out. The interior is a restless hall with a long reception counter, various job boards, and a back area dedicated to inspecting and buying monster materials

Arbalest Public Library

A large, prominent red building with an open gate, serving as a repository for knowledge and a study space for Royal Academy students

The Royal Academy of Arbalest

A prestigious complex set behind tall white gates in the central ward. Key facilities include:

  • Main Building and Lobby: A wide semi-indoor hall with high ceilings and stone pillars used for registration and orientation.

  • S-Class Homeroom: A semi-circular classroom with tiered rows of seats rising toward the back.

  • Magic Training Area/Field: A massive open space at least three times the size of a soccer field, equipped with magic crystals to maintain defensive barriers during spell practice.

  • Physical Training Area: A wide stretch of hardened earth divided into stone-marked rings for sparring and drills.

  • Private Training Room: A restricted facility containing weapon racks, practice dummies, and targets used for disciple-level training.

  • S-Class Dormitory: A lavish residence that resembles a palace, featuring a lobby with a large chandelier and polished granite floors. Elrond’s suite includes a wide bed, a private bathroom with a large bathtub, and a balcony

Main Building and Lobby (Royal Academy)

A wide semi-indoor hall with high ceilings and stone pillars used for registration and orientation within Royal Academy

Magic Training Area (Academy)

A massive open field at least three times the size of a soccer field, surrounded by low walls and spectator tiers. It is equipped with magic crystals that form protective barriers for high-output spell practice and formal duels.

Physical Training Area (Academy)

A stretch of hardened earth divided into stone-marked rings for weapon drills and sparring. It includes specialized private training rooms accessible only with faculty permission for disciple-level instruction.

S-Class Dormitory

A lavish section of the Academy dorms that resembles a royal palace, featuring polished granite floors and a large chandelier. The private rooms are suites with white granite tiles, private balconies, and full bathrooms with large bathtubs.

Royal Academy Dormitory

The Royal Academy dormitory stands near the main entrance gates, appearing more like a stately noble residence than a typical school building. Its orderly grounds feature a small park with a fountain and benches shaded by trees where students gather to talk or practice light spells. The interior begins with a bright entrance hall anchored by a central reception desk where staff monitor student ledgers. While standard student housing is located on the upper floors, the building is physically split; the general dormitory sections occupy one side, while the elite quarters are hidden away on the lowest level.

The Southern Frontier

The rural region where Elrond was raised, known for its peaceful but dangerous outskirts. It is an area characterized by agricultural hubs and heavy monster influence from the nearby wilderness.

Astrea Home

A two-story wooden house in the southern outskirts. The second floor houses Selene’s magic research study, a room packed with research notes, scrolls, and floating crystals used for developing regional barrier systems.

Eastern Woods

A notorious and unpredictable forest filled with towering trees and tangled roots. It is a dangerous habitat where monsters are often pushed out of the deep woods and into conflict with humans. Within these woods lies the Secret River Clearing, where a flat boulder overhangs a fast-moving river used by Elrond for chantless practice.

Eastern Magic Stone Site

An ancient formation of weathered stones that serves as the heart of the regional defensive barrier protecting southern towns. It contains a small wooden shelter for mages to rest after performing complex enchantment repairs.

Calderidge

An agricultural hub town governed by House Reynhardt as a vassal to the Alfrost family

Roxarea

The Crossroads Country A small "crossroads" nation positioned at the northern buffer zone, ruled by the immortal Elder Elf Serelis Thalanor. It is a major trade nexus known for its fertile valleys and irrigated orchards of pomegranate-like fruit used for high-value liquor.

Kingdom of Radras

The Red Coast A maritime monarchy on the western coast known for its powerful navy and shipyards. Its capital, Veylharbor, features shipyards and palaces carved directly into the coastal stone cliffs.

The Threshold Vault

A subterranean fortress buried on the southern rim of the Northern Wasteland within a "dead-mana" zone that interferes with scrying. Its surface is disguised as a collapsed frontier fort. Internally, it features looping geometry to disorient intruders and houses the Anchor Chamber, a sealed hall containing a monolith that opens a portal to a Pocket Dimension for meetings of the Seven Chaos Bringers.

The Divine Realm

A bright, endless white void where Saitou met the Watchers after his death. It is a place where time does not move and souls are prepared for reincarnation.

King Aldren von Arbalest II

- Title: King of the Althania Kingdom, Head of House Arbalest

- Age: Late 40s

- Appearance: Bright golden hair and vivid blue eyes (Darius’s mirror), tall and broad-shouldered; a calm, battle-forged presence with a faint scar along the jawline

- Personality: Principled, pragmatic, and quietly intimidating; prefers clear laws over court games; stern as a ruler, unexpectedly warm as a father

- Background:

Aldren earned the crown’s authority long before he wore it—through border wars, rebuilding ruined provinces, and forcing fractured nobles to accept reform. He became a king who believes stability is built with two hands: one holding a sword, the other holding a law.

Years ago, during a decisive campaign, Aldren was struck by a curse-borne sickness known within the Royal Academy as the Ashen Pulse. It has no cure—only management. The illness can lie dormant for weeks or months, then flare without warning: his heartbeat stutters, his limbs turn heavy, and his light magic becomes unstable. A severe flare can stop his heart outright, sudden and unforgiving.

Only a handful know the full truth of Aldren’s condition (the Queen, the royal physician, and a few sworn advisors). Publicly, Aldren remains unshakable; privately, every lesson he gives Lucien carries the weight of a question he never asks aloud: “If I fall tomorrow, can you hold Althania together?”

- Notable Ability:

Wields refined swordsmanship and “royal-grade” light magic known as Sovereign Radiance—an aura that strengthens allies and disrupts dark spells. During an Ashen Pulse flare, this power can backlash, making him dangerous to defend and terrifying to lose.

She nurtured Claire’s magical talent with careful patience, teaching her control, shielding techniques, and the emotional discipline needed to smile while carrying unbearable responsibility. Where Aldren trains Lucien for leadership in daylight, Lysandra prepares him for the shadows of court—how to read a room, recognize a lie, and win without drawing blood.

With Darius, her love is complicated: she sees his ambition as a wound left untreated, not evil by nature. She has quietly expanded palace wards and built a discreet network of informants within noble circles—not to destroy her son, but to prevent him from being consumed by forbidden influences.

- Notable Ability:

Master of protective magic and court-grade spellcraft—Silverweave Warding, a layered barrier style that can seal rooms, mute magic, and lock cursed artifacts without raising alarms.

History of Althania

Althania’s modern history begins with collapse. The old Althania royal bloodline ruled from a different capital until the great human–demon war reached its worst point. By then, the demons had already lost their single unifying leadership—the Demon Lords—killed by the Heroes of the past. Even without Demon Lords, the remaining demon armies were still strong enough to invade south, and in that invasion the Althania royal family was slaughtered, the capital burned, and the crown vanished. What followed became known as the Crownless Years: Althania had no king, and the demon race also had no Demon Lord, leaving both sides unstable—roads grew unsafe, monsters spread, and borderlands were left to survive on their own.

Out of that disorder, three noble houses moved first—**Arbalest, Roosevelt, and Alfrost**—later remembered as the Founding Families. They rallied scattered nobles and frontier garrisons, rebuilding an army from whatever the kingdom could still raise. The war only truly turned when two decisive forces joined them: the last descendants of the Heroes’ party (still carrying blessed blood) and the holy knights of the Western Church. Under one banner, humans pushed north, reclaiming towns and fortresses and driving the demon host back toward the wasteland border.

The end of the campaign became infamous as the Demon Massacre, when retreating demons were caught and crushed—especially once elven forces struck from other directions with superior magic and archery. The demons survived, but the loss was so severe—coming after the earlier fall of the Demon Lords—that they reshaped their rule: instead of risking everything on one Demon Lord, they created multiple Demon Lords, each controlling a region, army, and capital, making them harder to eliminate in one decisive heroic strike.

With the demons forced into the far north beyond a harsh wasteland/desert barrier, humans and elves chose diplomacy and alliance against the demon race. In the south, Althania rebuilt: House Arbalest took the throne, renamed the capital Arbalest, but kept the kingdom’s name Althania to honor the slain royal line. That peace endured for centuries—until the present era, where King Aldren von Arbalest II’s fatal curse-illness makes succession urgent, splitting the court into rival factions around Lucien and Darius, with Claire’s alignment capable of tipping the great houses and breaking the balance.

History of Radras

Radras is a maritime monarchy on the warm western coast of the central continent. Where Althania’s strength is its rebuilt institutions and disciplined land armies, Radras survives through ships, ports, contracts, and the ability to keep trade—and therefore food and steel—moving even when the continent burns. Its friendship with Althania is not sentimental; it is strategic, long-practiced, and mutually necessary.

1) THE RED COAST AND THE FIRST CROWN

Before it was a kingdom, Radras was a chain of rival coastal city-states wedged between iron-red cliffs and wide river deltas. Harbor lords feuded over docking rights, salt flats, and inland timber routes. Unification came under Rhadros the Oathbound, a warlord who conquered the coast and then bound it together through marriages and a permanent council that recorded obligations like law. That tradition became the Crown Chart: Radras’ habit of treating politics as written contract, not personal favor. The monarchy endures because it never forgets that ports only stay rich when peace is maintained.

2) RADRAS IN THE GREAT HUMAN–DEMON WAR

When the human–demon war intensified, Radras learned that demons didn’t need to conquer farmland to cripple a realm—they only had to choke routes. Inland roads became unsafe as monsters spread, and coastal raids turned frequent. Radras responded with sea-born doctrine: beacon-forts along cliff lines, the Waveguard navy to fight pirates and monsters, and mage-service embedded into sailing (ward-scribes, stormbreakers, signal-lantern mages).

At the same time, the wider demon race had already suffered a crucial blow: the Demon Lords had been slain by the ancient Heroes, leaving demons powerful but less unified. Demon strategy turned brutal and uneven—mass invasions and terror campaigns rather than clean, centralized conquest. Radras exploited that weakness by refusing “glory battles.” Instead, it protected arteries: it kept ports open, escorted grain ships, and ensured the continent still had routes that worked.

3) THE CROWNLESS YEARS AND THE QUIET ALLIANCE WITH ALTHANIA

When Althania’s old royal line was slaughtered and its capital burned, refugees poured out—commoners, minor nobles, and displaced soldiers. Many landed in Radras’ harbors. Radras did not openly seize Althania’s ruined lands; occupying shattered inland territory would be costly, and Radras’ court feared something worse than expansion: a fractured human south that would invite another demon surge.

So Radras helped without making itself the story.

- Merchant houses quietly funneled grain, iron, and timber into frontier markets that still resisted.

- Diplomats hosted “trade arbitrations” that were really negotiations between displaced Althanian nobles and surviving commanders.

- The Western Church received safe coastal passage, helping holy knights move where they were needed.

By the time Arbalest, Roosevelt, and Alfrost began rallying the remnants of Althania, Radras was already treating their coalition like a future state—supplying it, legitimizing it, and warning other human powers not to prey on the ruins.

4) AFTER THE DEMON MASSACRE: WHY RADRAS CLUNG TO ALTHANIA

When the counteroffensive finally drove demon forces north and the wasteland slaughter ended the campaign, the demons adapted in a way that alarmed Radras’ strategists: instead of one Demon Lord, they created multiple Demon Lords, each ruling a separate region with its own army and capital. That system made the north harder to predict—several threats could rise at once, each using different tactics: raids, corruption, sabotage, or “peace” offers meant to divide human courts.

Radras concluded that survival required alliances that outlast individual rulers. This is when the modern Radras–Althania relationship hardened into a formal framework often called the Harbor-Crown Accord:

A) Mutual Defense Understanding

Radras provides ships, coastal mobility, and supplies when demon pressure rises; Althania provides disciplined land forces and command structure.

B) Trade as Infrastructure

Radras becomes Althania’s door to the sea. Althania becomes Radras’ safest inland corridor. Both invest in keeping roads, escort routes, and border garrisons functional.

C) Permanent Diplomacy

Radras establishes a long-term embassy in Arbalest (nicknamed the Red Quarters for its dyes and banners), staffed not only by nobles but by contract-scribes trained to lock promises into airtight language. Althania returns the gesture with a resident envoy in Radras’ cliff-palace capital, Veylharbor, where shipyards are carved directly into stone.

Over generations, marriage ties form—not always direct heirs, but enough that both courts treat the alliance as “family-adjacent.” Even when other kingdoms try to pull Radras away, Radras answers with the same logic: Althania is the shield that keeps the south from becoming a battlefield again.

5) RADRAS TODAY

Radras is polite, cultured, and endlessly negotiable—until the ink dries. Its diplomacy favors written commitments and carefully framed concessions. A Radras envoy will smile while trapping rivals inside their own wording, and Radras guilds can move supplies faster than some kingdoms can raise levies.

Radras’ strengths:

- Navy-first power that can reposition troops and cargo quickly.

- Merchant guild influence integrated into governance.

- Information networks that hear rumors before palaces do.

Radras stays close to Althania because the age of one Demon Lord is gone; the age of many is harder to contain. If Althania fractures under internal succession strain, demons may not need a direct invasion—agents, timing, and divided humans could do the work.

If Althania is the rebuilt crown of the south, Radras is the anchor beside it—steadying the region so the next storm doesn’t take them both.

Divine Blessing

Divine Blessing is a power category distinct from ordinary learned magic. In the current codex, blessings originate from the Divine Beings during reincarnation: when a soul is sent into a new life, its old memories are normally erased, but a Blessing remains as the distilled trace of that past life’s talent, virtue, instinct, or affinity. Most people do not understand the source of their blessing. They only feel that a certain ability is strangely natural to them, as if the body remembers something the mind does not. Because of that, a blessing behaves less like a spell someone studies and more like an inborn authority built into the person from birth. Some blessings sharpen perception, some alter the body, and some bend rules that normal magic usually has to obey. They can be passive, always active, or difficult to control, and in rare cases they interact directly with other blessings rather than following normal magical counterplay. The current material also shows that blessings are not limited to the natural reincarnation cycle. Artificial blessings can be branded into a person from outside, creating abnormal “gifted” individuals whose power is deliberate rather than fated. Politically, Divine Blessings matter because they distort the normal balance of strength. A single blessed person can change security, warfare, succession, and factional power. That is why figures like Darius actively gather blessed individuals, while others treat blessings with caution, secrecy, or reverence. Elrond remains the biggest anomaly so far: he possesses a Divine Blessing, but its nature is still unknown even to the Divine Beings, and his retained past-life memories make his case fundamentally abnormal.
