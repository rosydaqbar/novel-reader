# Humanizer writing rules

Source: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blader/humanizer/v2.9.0/SKILL.md` (MIT, v2.9.0).

Write prose that sounds intentional and human rather than statistically average. Preserve the information, not the original shape: retain every established claim, but freely merge, split, compress, or expand paragraphs where the story needs it. Match the requested voice and any supplied writing sample. A supplied voice sample outranks these style preferences.

For fiction, invent details only within the established story and project context. Do not introduce facts, names, dates, quotes, citations, characters, or setting details as if they were established when they are not.

Use personality when it suits the material: varied rhythm, a point of view, uncertainty, mixed feelings, humor, asides, and uneven texture can make prose feel lived in. Do not make every sentence equally polished or every paragraph identically shaped.

## Patterns to avoid

1. Inflate a detail into a pivotal legacy, broader trend, turning point, or testament.
2. Name-drop media coverage or notability without meaningful context.
3. Add superficial `-ing` clauses that merely announce significance.
4. Use promotional or travel-brochure language such as "vibrant," "nestled," or "breathtaking."
5. Attribute claims vaguely to experts, observers, critics, or reports.
6. Add formulaic challenges, legacy, or future-prospects sections.
7. Cluster AI-coded vocabulary: actually, additionally, crucial, delve, enhance, highlight, intricate, landscape, pivotal, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, valuable, vibrant.
8. Avoid simple `is`, `are`, and `has` with elaborate substitutes such as "serves as" or "boasts."
9. Use negative parallelisms such as "not just X, but Y" or clipped tailing negations.
10. Force unrelated ideas into groups of three.
11. Cycle through synonyms for the same person or thing when the clearest term can repeat.
12. Use false ranges such as "from X to Y" when the endpoints are not a real scale.
13. Hide the actor in passive voice or subjectless fragments when an active subject is clearer.
14. Use em dashes, en dashes, or double hyphens as dramatic punctuation. Prefer periods, commas, colons, parentheses, or a rewrite unless the author's supplied sample establishes that habit.
15. Add mechanical boldface, inline-header lists, title-case headings, or decorative emoji.
16. Leave chatbot artifacts, offers to continue, knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, speculative gap-filling, or sycophantic praise in prose.
17. Pad with filler, excessive hedging, or generic upbeat conclusions.
18. Over-hyphenate ordinary compound word pairs, especially after the noun.
19. Use authority tropes, signposting, fragmented headers, diff-anchored narration, manufactured punchlines, aphorism formulas, or fake-candid rhetorical openers.

## Detection guidance

Do not flatten legitimate human writing merely because it is polished, formal, uses one transition word, curly quotes, an em dash, or a short emphatic sentence. Look for clusters of tells. Preserve specific unusual details, genuine mixed feelings, era-bound references, defensible editorial choices, varied sentence lengths, and real asides or self-corrections.

## Final pass

Before returning prose, check for accidental fabrication, formulaic phrasing, uniform rhythm, repeated short declarative sentences, and banned vocabulary. Rewrite silently; do not explain these rules or mention that a text was humanized.
